Antares First Launch - September 2026

Flight Software & Pad Operations

I wrote the flight computer software, covering telemetry transmission, flash/SD logging, and automatic parachute deployment logic. On launch day, I operated mission control, running health checks and making the flight readiness calls.

When telemetry cut out during the first launch window, I called an abort. Looking through the raw logs, I traced the issue to a bad PCB connector on the launch rail. The pad team re-seated the wiring, and we flew cleanly in the next window.

Post-Flight Debugging & Filter Tuning

When the parachute deployed early instead of at apogee, flight logs showed the variance on the estimated altitude being bigger than expected.

Since we didn't have enough time to get another launch opportunity in time, we mounted the avionics bay onto a drone in an attempt to recreate flight dynamics. Over three test flights, I made sure that acceleration and barometer updates are in sync, explicitly normalized the quaternions, and tuned the Extended Kalman Filter to handle rapid barometer pressure spikes.

Pre-Launch Crunch & Board Redesign (EUROC)

Two months before EUROC, RF compliance issues forced us to switch to a new V2 flight computer. With new PCBs being partially implemented only about 4 weeks before launch, I focused on shipping the absolute essentials: recovery logic, SD logging, and basic telemetry.

We had all core features hardware-tested two weeks ahead of launch. At the same time, three new developers joined the team, so I spent time onboarding them, breaking down tasks, and keeping the software pipeline moving under tight deadlines.
